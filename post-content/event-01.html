About the event

The OutKept Phishathon 2025, organised by OutKept and sponsored by Savaco, was a hands-on social engineering competition that pushed participants well beyond typical lab exercises. It ran as an on-site, time-pressured event aimed at students and early-career professionals with an interest in offensive security. Most cybersecurity events stay theoretical. This one dropped us straight into realistic phishing simulations, including a public-figure scenario and a deepfake task that demanded careful planning, ethical consideration, and clean execution.

What was covered

The event was built around targeted phishing simulations of increasing complexity. Each scenario needed a different approach: researching the target, building a convincing pretext, and executing without relying on cheap tricks. The deepfake challenge stood out the most, because it was not only a technical task. It was a question of where the line sits between a realistic simulation and an ethical overstep. Working through it forced me to think like an attacker planning a campaign and a defender who has to anticipate one.

My takeaway

The biggest lesson was about method. In social engineering, the quality of your research and the strength of your pretext matter far more than any flashy tool. A well-constructed, targeted approach beats a generic attack every time. That holds in a competition, and it holds in a real penetration testing engagement. I had already touched on social engineering during the penetration testing work in my studies, but competing under time pressure against other people made the lesson land in a way a classroom never could.

My verdict

This was one of the most practical events I attended this year. It reinforced what we study in the cybersecurity programme at Howest, in a way no classroom exercise can fully replicate. If you are a cybersecurity student who wants to get serious about social engineering, and to see how you hold up when the clock is running, this is the kind of event worth chasing down.
