About the event

This Tech & Meet learning session at Howest examined DeepSeek and its recent advances, aimed at a broad audience of students and tech enthusiasts who were curious about where AI is heading. The focus was on practical implications: how next-generation models are redefining performance benchmarks while also improving accessibility, computational efficiency, and responsible development.

What was covered

The lecture explored how DeepSeek's approach is reshaping parts of the AI landscape through a focus on compute efficiency and openness. Dimitri covered how optimisation is becoming a central pillar of modern AI research. The question is no longer only what a model can do, but how efficiently it can do it and who can access it. The discussion also touched on responsible AI development and what transparency actually means in practice for large model releases. A particularly valuable segment was the live demonstration of running a large language model entirely on a local machine.

My takeaway

As someone in cybersecurity, AI developments like this matter to me on two levels. The obvious one is that AI capabilities are advancing faster than most organisations can track, and that creates new attack surfaces and new tools for both defenders and attackers. The subtler one is that the efficiency and accessibility trend means increasingly more powerful AI models are reaching more actors, which has real implications for the threat landscape. The live local-model demo drove that home. If a capable model can run on a single machine, capability is no longer gated behind a large provider, and defenders have to assume that both well-resourced and low-resourced actors can attack.

AI capabilities are evolving faster than ever. The conversation is shifting from raw performance to efficiency, accessibility, and responsibility.

My verdict

A very interesting session that kept me engaged with the practical and strategic implications of these constantly evolving tools. Dimitri brought real expertise and thoughtful insights, and made space for a good discussion afterwards. His talk left us with concrete takeaways, both technical and conceptual. I would recommend it to anyone trying to make sense of how quickly AI is moving right now.
