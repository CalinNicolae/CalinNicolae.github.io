About the event

This tech meetup focused on the birth and evolution of NATO's cyber defence strategy. This is a topic that sits right at the intersection of cybersecurity, geopolitics, and international cooperation. The session traced the journey from early collaborative frameworks to today's coordinated multinational cyber readiness programmes.

What was covered

The speaker walked through how rapidly the cyber threat landscape has transformed over the past two decades, and how an organisation like NATO has had to fundamentally rethink what defence means when the battlefield extends into the digital domain. The discussion covered early NATO cyber initiatives, the key incidents that accelerated strategic change, and where current multinational coordination efforts stand today.

My takeaway

Three things stayed with me after this session. First, that cyber defence is now a core component of global security strategy and not a supporting function or an afterthought. Second, that meaningful defence at a global scale requires collaboration between nations, private companies, and researchers in a way that no single organisation can replicate alone. Third, and most relevant to me personally, is that the future of defence will be shaped as much by policy and education as by technology. After this, I am convinced that engineers and policymakers need a shared grasp of how things work in each others jobs.

Cybersecurity is no longer a siloed discipline, it is a shared responsibility across borders, sectors, and communities.

My verdict

This was one of the more thought-provoking events of the year for me. It broadened my perspective on what a career in cybersecurity can look like. This field extends well beyond technical roles into strategy, policy, and international collaboration. I left feeling motivated to keep growing in this space. Highly recommended for any security student who wants to understand the bigger picture.
