About the event

This free evening session at Howest covered the birth and evolution of NATO's cyber defence strategy, a topic that sits at the intersection of cybersecurity, geopolitics, and international cooperation. It traced the path from early collaborative frameworks to today's coordinated, multinational cyber readiness programmes.

What was covered

The session traced how quickly the cyber threat landscape has transformed over the past two decades, and how an organisation like NATO has had to fundamentally rethink what defence means when the battlefield extends into the digital domain. From early NATO cyber initiatives and the key incidents that accelerated strategic change to where multinational coordination stands today.

My takeaway

Three things stayed with me afterwards. First, cyber defence is now a core component of global security strategy, not a supporting function or an afterthought. Second, meaningful defence at a global scale needs collaboration between nations, private companies, and researchers in a way no single organisation can replicate alone. Third, and most relevant to me personally, the future of defence will be shaped as much by policy and education as by technology. We rarely see this strategic, geopolitical side of security in the programme, which is exactly why it was worth the evening. After this, I am convinced that engineers and policymakers need a shared grasp of how things work in each other's worlds.

Cybersecurity is no longer a siloed discipline, it is a shared responsibility across borders, sectors, and communities.

My verdict

This was one of the more thought-provoking events of the year for me. It widened my view of what a career in cybersecurity can look like, well beyond technical roles, into strategy, policy, and international work. I left motivated to keep growing in this direction. For any security student who wants the bigger picture and not just the tooling, this one is worth making time for.
