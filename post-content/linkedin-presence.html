Where I began

When I started this module, my LinkedIn profile only had a name and a picture. A fresh student account with no details and no real sense of who I am as a professional. The Professional Networking module forced me to change that, not by copying what everyone else does, but by actually thinking about what I want to communicate to the world.

The assignment was straightforward on the surface: develop a professional profile photo, craft a compelling bio, list my skills, detail my education and experience, and expand my network with at least 10 relevant connections. But it turned out to be much more than a checkbox exercise. It forced me to articulate my professional identity while I'm still figuring it out.

What I worked on

First came the practical elements. I updated my profile photo, something that seems trivial but matters more than I expected. A professional headshot signals that you take yourself seriously as a professional. I wrote a bio that tried to strike a balance between my student status and my real experience: three months at Eniris as an IoT intern, my focus on cybersecurity, and my genuine interest in how technology intersects with security and infrastructure.

I added my skills honestly: Python, networking fundamentals, security concepts, and system administration exposure without inflating what I actually know. I listed my education at HOWEST and made sure my internship experience was prominent, not buried. The internship at Eniris became the centerpiece; it represents the transition from theory to practice.

For expanding my network, I was strategic. I connected with classmates and lecturers from Howest, professionals I met at the various Tech & Meet events that I attended, speakers and organizers, and people working in cybersecurity and software development, both for Eniris and for other companies. I aimed for quality over quantity, I am currently at 153 connections at the time of writing this post.

Why this matters to me

What surprised me was how much the act of building my profile clarified my own thinking. Writing a bio forces you to answer questions you might otherwise avoid: Who am I as a professional right now? What do I want people to know about me? What story does my experience tell?

I also realized that LinkedIn isn't just a job board, it's also a window into how the professional community sees you. Every post I share about an event I attended or a lesson I learned becomes part of that image. My posts about NATO cyber defence, IPv6, DeepSeek, and my internship ending aren't just documentation; they're statements about what I care about and what I'm learning.

The networking side was equally revealing. Connecting with people opened conversations I wouldn't have had otherwise. Some reached out after seeing my posts. Others I initiated contact with after an event. The module's emphasis on expanding my network wasn't about collecting contacts, it was about expanding my professional network.

The bigger picture

I now see LinkedIn as a tool for continuous professional communication. Every event I attend, every project I work on, every lesson I learn can become material for sharing. It's about self-promotion, but it's also about demonstrating that I'm engaged, learning, and contributing to conversations that matter in my field.

For a cybersecurity student preparing to enter the job market, this profile serves a real purpose. It shows employers and peers that I don't just know theory. I attend events, I reflect on what I learn, I connect with professionals, and I think critically about the future of tech. It signals engagement with the industry beyond the classroom.

My verdict

This assignment felt smaller than it actually was. Building a LinkedIn profile seemed like administrative overhead, but it turned out to be one of the most clarifying exercises in this module. It forced me to think about my professional identity, to take my experience seriously, and to see networking not as small talk but as potential long-term relationships.

If you're a student hesitating over whether to "bother" with LinkedIn: don't hesitate. It matters. Not because it's a requirement, but because it's the space where your professional presence lives. Make it authentic, keep it updated, and use it to document your growth. The connections you build now will still be there after graduation.
