About the event

.NET 10 Demystified was a technical evening session organised together with Howest, bringing developers, architects, and engineers into one room. The audience was a mix of working professionals and students, which gave it an industry feel rather than a classroom one. The focus stayed practical the whole way through. It was less about feature announcements and more about what actually changes when you sit down to write and ship code.

What was covered

The session went deep on the newest .NET 10 features, with the emphasis on applying them in real projects instead of toy examples. Topics included performance tuning, building maintainable APIs, and designing cloud-ready systems. What kept my attention was the focus on trade-offs: when a new feature is genuinely worth adopting and when it is not, instead of treating every change as an automatic upgrade. The speaker leaned heavily on his own production experience, which kept the content useful rather than academic.

My takeaway

As a cybersecurity student, I came at this from a different angle than most of the room. I was less interested in the framework features themselves and more in the security implications of the architectural patterns being shown. Maintainable APIs and cloud-ready systems are exactly the infrastructure I will have to assess and secure, so understanding how developers build them makes me a better tester. The networking side was valuable too. Talking to architects and senior developers gave me a clearer picture of how software teams actually work day to day.

My verdict

For someone interested in both security and software development, this was time well spent, and a good reminder that cybersecurity does not exist in isolation. Understanding the tools and patterns developers use every day is part of the job. I would point any student who wants a fuller picture of the software side of the field toward a session like this one.
